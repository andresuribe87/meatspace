# meatspace strategy memo: sim-to-real verification

**Date:** 2026-11-01 · **From:** Head of Product · **Status:** draft for founder decision · **Fact-checked:** 49 citation checks, 31 supported, 18 corrected (no fabricated sources)
**Inputs:** five research lenses (landscape, demand, technical, agents, supply, business), a red-team review, and a synthetic customer panel. **The panel is agent-generated personas, not real interviews. Treat its numbers as hypotheses.**

---

## 1. Bottom line

- **The problem is real.** Real-robot evidence is still what counts, and it costs a lot of labor. One OpenVLA comparison took more than 100 human-hours for about 2,500 rollouts ([AutoEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.24278v2)). **The founder's version of the product is structurally a benchmark, though.** You can only verify a sim result on a real setup that matches it. Researchers without hardware already get matched real-robot benchmarks for free or through non-commercial access: [RoboArena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.18123) (sponsored free budget), [AutoEval](https://auto-eval.github.io/) (open access, no fee stated; 2026 tasks "TBD"), [RoboChallenge](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17950v1) (public remote access, pricing not published), [RobotReplica](https://robot-replica.org/) (non-commercial, no fees stated). That segment's willingness to pay is about $0.
- **The pain that comes with a budget sits elsewhere.** Three groups: (a) teams that own robots and are buried in evaluation labor; (b) sim and world-model vendors who need independent real ground truth; (c) startups that need third-party numbers on an embodiment they don't own. We found no published price and no named paying customer for any of these ([Positronic roundup](https://blog.positronic.ro/best-real-robot-policy-endpoint-evaluation-vendors-2026)).
- **Recommended wedge: twin-first verification.** We publish a calibrated, version-pinned sim twin of a specific real cell. The twin is free and has the same API as the real cell. Running on the real cell is the paid "verify" step. The product is agent-native (artifacts, not teleop; MCP Tasks; hard spend caps). We start with software only: twin gap reports built from public LeRobot data.
- **No hardware purchase until at least $3k is committed.** The 90-day bar: 3 paid pilots of $1k or more (at least 1 B2B), 2 LOIs of $10k/yr or more, and at least 10 external groups using the twin. If we miss it, we either flip to selling `meatspaced` to people who already own robots, or we stop.
- **This week, remove the invented traction, USDC escrow and "TPM-signed" claims from meatspace.sh.** For a verification business, credibility is the product.

## 2. The problem, precisely

**Definition.** A sim result "transfers" when the same policy, on a physically matched setup, reaches the same outcome within a stated confidence interval. The gap breaks down into actuator dynamics and latency, contact physics, perception, scene and object distribution, and the latency of inference over the network.

**Evidence the gap matters**

| Signal | Source |
| --- | --- |
| CoRL 2026 asks for real-robot results *or* "convincing evidence" that sim transfers. Real results are encouraged, not required. | [CoRL CFP](https://2026.corl.org/contributions/call-for-papers) |
| Real evaluation is "impractical for individual groups … due to cost and labor"; sim "does not entirely replace real-world evaluation" | [PolaRiS](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16881v2) |
| "No simulator perfectly reproduces the physical world." Agents needed real rollouts to repair the sim. | [SimEX](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38982) |
| RoboChallenge's authors report that with the same props, task and model, measured success can swing from 0% to 100% run to run; who resets the scene also changes success rates materially | [RoboChallenge](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17950v1) |
| World-model evaluators were validated against 1,600+ real trials. Their builders still needed the real data. | [Veo evaluator](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10675) |

**Statistical power is part of the problem.** These are our Wilson 95% intervals at p=0.5:

| Trials (n) | 95% interval | Is it "verification"? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | 24–76% | No |
| 50 | 37–63% | Only a smoke test |
| ~194 | detects a 10-pt sim-vs-real gap (80% power) | Yes |
| ~388 per arm | separates two real policies 10 pts apart | Yes |

**Our own zero-hardware finding** (internal replay experiment, 2026-11-01). We replayed 50 real episodes of [lerobot/svla_so101_pickplace](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lerobot/svla_so101_pickplace) in the [MuJoCo Menagerie SO-101 twin](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco_menagerie/tree/main/robotstudio_so101). The real follower lags its command by about 4 frames (~133 ms); the stock twin lags by about 1 frame. Per-joint sim-vs-real RMSE is 3.2–6.2°. The likely cause is that the stock twin's position gains (kp=998.22, kv=2.731) are too stiff; the MJCF derives them from an assumed servo P gain and says they are "not a 1-to-1 mapping" of LeRobot's servo gains. **Caveats:** the dataset's metadata lists `robot_type: so100_follower` despite the so101 name, so the match to the SO-101 twin is assumed, not confirmed; part of the twin's lag comes from the order of control and stepping in our replay; the real lag includes teleop pipeline latency; units were linearly mapped because the dataset ships without its calibration file; final-pose gaps are under 1°. We have not shown any effect on task success. **Counterpoint:** the same replay on [lerobot/svla_so100_pickplace](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lerobot/svla_so100_pickplace) (degrees, no unit mapping) against Menagerie's `trs_so_arm100` twin shows the stock twin already close to real: comparable lag (3–4 frames both) and 0.3–1.7° sim-vs-real RMSE. Gap size depends on the twin and the data, which is exactly what a gap report should measure.

## 5. The offer (v0.2)

Three SKUs that share one report schema.

| SKU | Customer submits | Gets back | SLA | Price hypothesis (assumed) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Twin Gap Report** | HF Hub LeRobot dataset ID + robot type | Lag, per-joint RMSE, fitted twin parameters, downloadable twin package, hash | < 1 h, automated | Free for public datasets; $99–499 for private |
| **Twin Trials** (sim) | Policy-server endpoint + task ID + n | Sim success with Wilson CI, with measured real latency injected | Minutes | Free, rate-limited |
| **Real Verification** | Same request with `mode: real`, optionally the customer's own sim results | Real-vs-twin delta with CIs, minimum detectable effect, signed bundle | Concierge: 72h per 50 trials. Target p95 < 30 min per 10-trial batch only after auto-reset exists. | $3–5/ep standard; $1–3k academic study / $2–5k commercial (200 trials); $10–25k calibration audit; academics get 20 free real eps/mo |

**Request** (keeps v0.1's NDJSON streaming and spend caps):

```json
POST /v1/trials
{ "cell": "so101-pick-v1", "mode": "sim",
  "policy": {"server": "grpc://host:port", "protocol": "lerobot-async"},
  "n_trials": 200, "max_spend_usd": 400,
  "your_sim": {"checkpoints": ["a","b"], "success": [0.62, 0.71]},
  "idempotency_key": "..." }
```

**Report fields**

- Real and sim success with Wilson CIs, the gap with its CI, and the minimum detectable effect.
- Pearson and MMRV correlation when there are 3 or more checkpoints ([SIMPLER metrics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05941)).
- Joint and end-effector RMSE from matched initial states.
- Command-to-state lag and a latency histogram.
- **Safety-clamp counts**, because clamps change the outcome.
- A failure taxonomy, video, MCAP, and a LeRobotDataset v3 export.
- An Ed25519-signed manifest of artifact hashes. **We drop "TPM" language until we have real hardware attestation.**

**Rules from the evidence**

- **No customer code or weights run on our machines.** The customer hosts a policy server (the AutoEval/RoboArena/RoboChallenge pattern). This also removes IP concerns.
- **No live LLM teleop.** SimEX's online-control baseline finished 0 of 15 rollouts ([SimEX](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38982)).
- **No "verified" label below n=50.** The default for a gap claim is 200 trials.
- **Signing does not prevent gaming.** The reported RoboArena incident was evaluator capture, fixed through governance ([huxiu](https://www.huxiu.com/article/4869933.html?type=text), weakly sourced). Blind scheduling and evaluators with no stake in the result matter more.

**Agent interface (thin, and only as far as the evidence supports)**

- An MCP server on the [Tasks extension](https://aaif.io/blog/handle-long-running-mcp-tool-calls-with-the-mcp-tasks-extension) with tools `list_cells`, `get_twin`, `quote`, `submit_trials` (returns a task) and `get_results`. Above an org-set spend threshold it returns `input_required`.
- Scoped API keys with hard caps the agent cannot raise. The org is billed by card or credits.
- Agents call the service; they are not the buyers. x402's headline activity (~75M transactions, $24M over 30 days) is roughly half artificial; real volume is only about $28–40k/day ([Forkast](https://forkast.news/half-of-x402s-transaction-volume-is-artificial-the-infrastructure-layer-keeps-building-anyway/)).
- **We build no agent-specific features until an agent operator pays.**

## 6. Supply strategy

| Option | Precedent | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Own identical cells | AutoEval, RRC, PhAIL: every working service | **Yes, gated.** Start at 1 cell, grow to 3 to measure the noise floor. |

| Open marketplace of idle third-party robots | No research precedent. **Sequencing**

1. **Days 0–60: no owned hardware.** Supply is public datasets, plus a request to RobotReplica's UT Dallas SO-101 site about concierge runs. Whether they would serve a commercial party is unverified.
2. **Gate 1 (≥$3k committed):** one founder-run SO-101 cell, under $1k ([kit $220–240](https://www.cnx-software.com/2025/05/02/so-arm101-open-source-dual-robotic-arm-kit-works-with-hugging-faces-lerobot/)), then 3 identical cells at roughly $3–8k.
3. **Gate 2 (≥$10k LOI on a credible embodiment):** a Franka FR3 ([~$29k](https://vention.io/parts/franka-robotics-fr3-v2-4193)) or a YAM pair ([~$3k each](https://www.roboticscenter.ai/store/product/i2rt-yam-6dof), ~$6k per pair), depending on who signed.
4. **Drones, quadrupeds and industrial sites stay out of scope.** They carry the highest liability, and FAA Part 108 is unsettled ([UAV Coach](https://uavcoach.com/part-108-stuck/)).

**Twin Gap Report v1 spec**

- **Datasets:** [lerobot/svla_so101_pickplace](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lerobot/svla_so101_pickplace) (50 eps, 11,939 frames) and [lerobot/svla_so100_pickplace](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lerobot/svla_so100_pickplace) (50 eps, 19,631 frames). Then the 5 most-downloaded of the ~5k [so100](https://huggingface.co/api/datasets?filter=so100&sort=downloads) / ~2k [so101](https://huggingface.co/api/datasets?filter=so101&sort=downloads) Hub datasets (5,011 / 2,062 on 2026-11-01). Then DROID (Franka, ships its camera calibration; [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12945v2)); feasibility unverified.
- **Twins:** Menagerie `robotstudio_so101` and `trs_so_arm100` on MuJoCo 3.17. Later, `franka_emika_panda` for DROID.
- **Method:** replay the recorded `action` into the twin's position actuators at 30 fps, open loop, and compare against `observation.state`. Gripper excluded. Fix the control-then-step ordering bias first.
- **Metrics:**
  - per-joint command-to-state lag (cross-correlation, in frames and ms);
  - per-joint sim-vs-real RMSE (mean and p90, degrees);
  - real vs sim tracking RMSE;
  - fitted kp, damping, armature, friction loss and pure delay by least squares on the replays ([BAM](https://bam.readthedocs.io/en/latest/) identifies damping, friction loss, armature and delay, but treats kp as a known firmware input and expects test-bench logs);
  - **held-out RMSE and lag, fitting on 40 episodes and testing on 10**.
- **What counts as a genuine finding (our threshold, assumed):** the fitted twin cuts held-out RMSE by at least 50% and matches lag within 1 frame, consistently across 3 or more datasets from different owners.
- **Honest scope:** actuator and proprioception level only. No contact, vision or task-success claims, and it does not verify new policies. The red team notes anyone can fit kp and latency in an afternoon. The value is in doing it at scale, publishing it, and standardizing it, not in the method.

## 11. Decisions that need the founder

1. **Who is the customer?**
   - Options: (A) researchers and agents without hardware, through twin-first verification; (B) robot owners, with `meatspaced` sold as evaluation-automation software; (C) test both for 30 days.
   - **Recommendation: C.** Build A, test B through interviews only, and flip at day 30 if B gets more commitments.
   - *Why it's yours:* B abandons the "research without hardware" mission for a customer with better evidence behind it.
2. **Will you run a physical operation?**
   - Options: never (software-only company) / gated / buy 3 cells now.
   - **Recommendation: gated.** No hardware before $3k is committed; a Franka only after a $10k LOI.
   - *Why it's yours:* it commits your time, a location, liability and insurance.
3. **First real embodiment once a gate is hit?**
   - Options: SO-101 (twin and community, low credibility) / bimanual YAM (agent research) / Franka DROID (B2B credibility, $29k+).
   - **Recommendation:** SO-101 for the software phase; the first purchase follows whoever signs the first LOI.
   - *Why it's yours:* capex runs from $1k to $30k, and the choice decides which community we join.
